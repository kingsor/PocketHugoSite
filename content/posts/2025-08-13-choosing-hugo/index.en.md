For a while, I used [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com/) for my blog, which is originally supported by default on [GitHub Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages). It’s a solid [static site generator](https://jamstack.org/generators/), but it’s complicated to test locally on Windows unless you use [Docker](https://github.com/BretFisher/jekyll-serve).

So I looked for something else and eventually settled on [Hugo](https://gohugo.io/), which is very user-friendly, written in [Go](https://go.dev/), supports pages and posts written in [Markdown](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markdown), and is very fast at generating the site locally for testing before publishing.

It has excellent [support](https://gohugo.io/documentation/) on its website and on [Stack Overflow](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/tagged/hugo), and offers numerous [free themes](https://themes.gohugo.io/) to choose from if you’re unable or unwilling to create your own.

GitHub Pages [supports publishing a site with Hugo](https://gohugo.io/host-and-deploy/host-on-github-pages/), so it’s very convenient to write blog posts locally, test the results locally, make any necessary corrections, and—once you’re satisfied—commit and push to the relevant repository, then see your blog updated online within a few minutes.
